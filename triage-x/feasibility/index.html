About this page

This page collects publicly releasable summaries, illustrations and video of capability demonstrated by CMU Team Chiron. Each entry links to a short document, an illustration, or a video. Further material is published here as it is prepared for release.

Detailed technical reports and program data are not hosted here. Distribution-limited material, raw datasets and scored-run records are available to authorized reviewers on request — please contact darpa-triage@cmu.edu.

Capability Evidence

C1 Evidence posted

Casualty detection and localization

Autonomously find and localize casualties and report their location to within 2 m.

During a human-machine teaming run at the DARPA Triage Challenge Final Event, the air–ground team found and localized 44 casualties, placing each one on a shared map. Mapping, detection, localization and close-range inspection all ran in real time: alongside the casualties, the team reconstructed a 3D map of the whole area on the fly, which small UAVs and ground robots then used to plan routes and avoid obstacles.

Real-time 3D mapping, casualty detection, localization and inspection. Human-machine teaming day run, DARPA Triage Challenge Final Event, 12 November 2026.

C3 Evidence posted

Standoff vital signs

Perform accurate standoff vital-signs assessment, including at minimum heart rate and respiratory rate.

A 120 GHz FMCW radar carried on a Spot quadruped measures heart and respiratory rate with no contact and no line of sight to the face, at 1.7–4.3 m standoff. Respiratory rate is reliable across 79 standoff trials; heart rate at robotic standoff is markedly harder than at clinical close range, and the report quantifies that gap rather than averaging over it. At the DARPA Triage Challenge Finals Gate 4, two autonomous Spot robots completed standoff vital-signs assessment on all eight outdoor casualties.

C7 Evidence posted

Diverse, realistic environments

Operate indoors and outdoors, daytime and nighttime; adverse weather (rain, smoke) desirable but not required.

Reaching casualties inside a structure begins at the building envelope. A lightweight Starling 2 UAV autonomously detects an opening in a wall, aligns itself to it, approaches, inspects the interior from the opening, and retreats safely. The aircraft looks in from the opening rather than flying through it.

Starling 2 UAV approaching a wall opening for casualty inspection. Allegheny County Fire Academy, fall 2026.

C8 Evidence posted

Human-interpretable graphical output

Produce a human-interpretable output through a graphical interface conveying casualty locations and priorities of care.

Robots search first; medics arrive minutes later and need to find a specific casualty. Track over Chiron (ToC) is a purpose-built Android app, carried on a chest-mounted phone, that reads the robot fleet's live database through the base station. It shows every detected casualty on a satellite map beside a casualty list carrying each one's triage priority, and navigates the medic to a chosen casualty with a distance and heading overlay alongside that casualty's photograph and the findings the fleet reported.

Track over Chiron (ToC), the medic-facing Android app: casualty map, casualty list and navigation to a selected casualty. Prototype.
